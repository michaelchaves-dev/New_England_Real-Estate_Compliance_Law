# Product split — free renters / paid operators

Decision recorded September 13, 2026. Clarified the same night.

## Non-negotiable

Nothing in this project may shrink, dodge, or “optimize around” tenant rights or protections. If a commercial feature would make an illegal lockout, illegal fee, deposit theft, retaliation, or fair-housing violation easier, it does not ship.

Compliance is the product. Tenant protections are the floor, not an obstacle.

## Free (public, all six New England states)

Renters get the research at no charge:

- State rights reports in `states/`
- Comparison snapshot and renter-help directory in `resources/`
- Official handbook and legal-aid links
- Plain-language checklists

No account required. No paywall on tenant-rights text.

## Paid (commercial operators)

Landlords, property-management companies, brokerages, and lawyers already have a duty to follow these rules. They also profit from operating rental housing. The paid layer exists because **staying compliant is expensive in time**: session laws move, cities overlay the baseline, deposit clocks and fee bans differ by state, and a missed deadline is a damages statute.

What they buy is not a loophole. They buy:

- Outsourced research and statute-watch so their team does not rebuild the six-state matrix every session
- Jurisdiction packs: lawful notice paths, deposit ledgers, fee-ban audits, disclosure checklists
- Change-log / watch-list when effective dates land
- Portfolio gap analysis (city overlay + subsidy + lead + registration)
- Time, attention, and error-rate back — so compliance is the default operating system instead of a weekend fire drill

Pricing and access control live outside this public repo.

## Hard line

This repository is **not legal advice** and is **not a substitute for a barred attorney**. The free layer educates renters. The paid layer sells **currency of compliance** to people who earn from the housing. It does not sell evasion.
