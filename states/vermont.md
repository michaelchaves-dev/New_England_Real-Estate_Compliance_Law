# Vermont Tenant Rights Report

**Scope:** Vermont residential renter rights and landlord compliance duties, current as of **July 30, 2026**. This is an informational compliance report, not legal advice.

## Executive Summary

Vermont renter protections are mainly in the **Residential Rental Agreements Act, 9 V.S.A. Chapter 137**, plus the Vermont Rental Housing Health Code, fair housing law, and local rules in places such as Burlington and Barre. The major tenant rights are: habitable housing, no application fees, 60-day statewide rent-increase notice, 14-day security deposit return, protection from illegal lockouts and utility shutoffs, court process before eviction, anti-retaliation protections, fair housing protections, and strict landlord-entry rules.

The big 2026 policy item is that Vermont’s major landlord-tenant reform bill, **H.772**, failed in the Senate after a tie-breaking vote in May 2026. That means proposed changes on eviction procedure, security deposit caps, rent-increase limits, and rental-payment credit reporting did not become statewide law this session. Sources: [Vermont Public](https://www.vermontpublic.org/), [Valley News](https://www.vnews.com/), [Vermont Legislature H.772](https://legislature.vermont.gov/bill/status/2026/H.772).

## 1. High Severity / Statutory Risk

### Application Fees Are Prohibited

A Vermont landlord or landlord’s agent may not charge an application fee for a residential rental application. This is a statewide rule under **9 V.S.A. § 4456a**. ([legislature.vermont.gov](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/09/137/04456a))

Practical action: landlords should not charge application, review, screening, or processing fees as a condition of applying for residential housing.

### Rent Increases Require Written Notice

A rent increase is not effective unless the tenant receives proper written notice. Statewide, the landlord must give at least **60 days’ written notice** before the increase takes effect. The notice must be mailed or hand-delivered. Burlington generally requires **90 days’ written notice**. ([VTLawHelp rent increases](https://www.vtlawhelp.org/rent-increases))

Practical action: use written rent-increase notices and check local rules before issuing increases.

### Security Deposits Must Be Returned Quickly

Vermont requires landlords to return the security deposit with a written itemized statement of deductions within **14 days** after the landlord discovers the tenant has vacated or abandoned the unit, or from the date the tenant vacated if the tenant gave notice of that date. Permitted deductions include unpaid rent, damage beyond normal wear and tear, unpaid utility or other charges the tenant owed, and costs to remove abandoned property. If the landlord misses the deadline, the landlord forfeits the right to withhold any part of the deposit; willful failure can mean double damages plus attorney’s fees and costs. ([9 V.S.A. § 4461](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/09/137/04461); [VTLawHelp security deposits](https://www.vtlawhelp.org/security-deposits))

Important correction: Vermont has **no statewide security deposit cap**, though Burlington and Barre have local deposit limits and interest rules. ([VTLawHelp security deposits](https://www.vtlawhelp.org/security-deposits); Burlington Housing Board of Review)

## 2. Habitability And Repairs

Vermont landlords must deliver and maintain rental housing that is safe, clean, fit for human habitation, and compliant with building, housing, and health regulations. ([9 V.S.A. § 4457](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/09/137/04457))

Minimum habitability expectations include working plumbing, potable water, hot water, safe wastewater disposal, safe electrical service, heating facilities, structural safety, and compliance with the Vermont Rental Housing Health Code. VTLawHelp summarizes that if the outdoor temperature is below **55 degrees**, the heating system must be able to maintain at least **65 degrees** in rooms. ([VTLawHelp repairs](https://www.vtlawhelp.org/repairs); Vermont rental housing code summary)

If a condition materially affects health or safety and the landlord fails to fix it after written notice and a reasonable time, a tenant may have remedies including rent withholding, filing in court for injunctive relief, damages, attorney’s fees, or ending the rental agreement. Tenants should be careful with rent withholding and get legal advice where possible. ([9 V.S.A. § 4458](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/09/137/04458); [VTLawHelp repairs](https://www.vtlawhelp.org/repairs))

For minor defects, tenants may be able to repair and deduct after notice, but the deduction generally may not exceed **one-half of one month’s rent**. ([9 V.S.A. § 4459](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/09/137/04459))

## 3. Landlord Entry And Tenant Privacy

A landlord may enter with the tenant’s consent, which cannot be unreasonably withheld. Without consent, a landlord may enter only for listed purposes such as inspection, repairs, alterations, improvements, services, or showing the unit, and must give at least **48 hours’ notice**. Entry must be between **9:00 a.m. and 9:00 p.m.**, unless an emergency or other statutory exception applies. ([9 V.S.A. § 4460](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/09/137/04460))

## 4. Eviction Rights And Notice Periods

A termination notice is only the first step. A tenant does not have to leave unless the tenant voluntarily moves or the landlord files an eviction case and wins a court order. ([VTLawHelp ending tenancy](https://www.vtlawhelp.org/ending-tenancy-and-moving-out); [Vermont Judiciary](https://www.vermontjudiciary.org/))

Common statewide notice periods:

| Ground | Notice period |
|--------|---------------|
| Nonpayment of rent | **14 days’** written notice |
| Material lease breach | Generally **30 days’** notice |
| Criminal activity, illegal drug activity, or violence threatening health/safety | **14 days’** notice |
| No-cause termination without written rental agreement | **60 days** if tenancy is two years or less; **90 days** if more than two years |
| No-cause termination at end of written rental agreement | **30 days** if tenancy is two years or less; **60 days** if more than two years |
| Sale of building with no written rental agreement | Generally **30 days** |

Burlington no-cause rules can be longer: VTLawHelp reports **90 days** if the tenant lived there less than two years and **120 days** if two years or more. ([9 V.S.A. § 4467](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/09/137/04467); [VTLawHelp ending tenancy](https://www.vtlawhelp.org/ending-tenancy-and-moving-out))

## 5. Illegal Evictions

Vermont prohibits self-help eviction. A landlord may not shut off utilities, lock a tenant out, deny access to the rented premises, or remove tenant property except through proper judicial process. ([9 V.S.A. § 4463](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/09/137/04463); [VTLawHelp](https://www.vtlawhelp.org/))

Practical action: lock changes, utility interruptions, or property removal should be treated as high-risk conduct unless authorized by court process or a true emergency repair situation.

## 6. Retaliation Protections

A landlord may not retaliate by changing rental terms, threatening eviction, or bringing an action because the tenant complained to a government agency about health/safety violations, complained to the landlord about violations of the rental law, or joined/organized a tenant association. ([9 V.S.A. § 4465](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/09/137/04465); [VTLawHelp rental agreements](https://www.vtlawhelp.org/rental-agreements))

Retaliation can be a defense in an eviction case. Property managers should document legitimate, non-retaliatory reasons for notices issued after tenant complaints.

## 7. Fair Housing And Discrimination

Vermont fair housing law protects renters from discrimination based on race, color, national origin, immigration status, citizenship, religion, pregnancy or living with children under 18, receipt of public assistance or benefits, sexual orientation, sex, gender identity, victim status related to domestic violence/stalking/sexual assault, disability, age, and marital status. ([VTLawHelp fair housing](https://www.vtlawhelp.org/fair-housing))

Federal fair housing law also protects against discrimination based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, and disability. ([HUD Fair Housing Act overview](https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/fair_housing_equal_opp/fair_housing_act_overview))

Practical action: rental ads, screening standards, occupancy rules, voucher/public-assistance treatment, disability accommodations, and service/emotional-support animal policies should be reviewed for fair housing compliance.

## 8. Local Overlay: Burlington And Barre

Burlington and Barre have local security deposit rules that are stricter than Vermont’s statewide baseline. The older Vermont renter handbook states that outside Burlington and Barre there is no statewide deposit cap and no statewide interest requirement; Burlington and Barre impose local deposit limits and interest rules. ([Renting in Vermont handbook](https://www.cvoeo.org/))

Burlington also has a Housing Board of Review and local security deposit procedures. The city says landlords must return the deposit within **14 days** from the date the tenant vacated, with a written statement itemizing deductions. ([Burlington Housing Board of Review](https://www.burlingtonvt.gov/))

## 9. Watch List / 2026 Legislative Background

**H.772** was the major 2026 landlord-tenant reform vehicle. It passed the House but failed in the Senate after a tie-breaking vote. As reported by Vermont Public, it contained both landlord-favorable eviction streamlining and renter-favorable provisions, including longer no-cause timeframes, limits on security deposits, and limits on how often rents could be increased. It did not become law. ([Vermont Public](https://www.vermontpublic.org/); [H.772 status](https://legislature.vermont.gov/bill/status/2026/H.772))

**S.309**, a related residential rental agreements and eviction procedures bill, was introduced and referred to Senate Judiciary but did not advance. ([S.309 status](https://legislature.vermont.gov/bill/status/2026/S.309))

## 10. Practical Compliance Checklist

For Vermont rental operations, confirm:

1. No application fees are charged.
2. Rent increases are written, mailed or hand-delivered, and use at least 60 days’ notice statewide or 90 days in Burlington.
3. Security deposit procedures use the 14-day return and itemization deadline.
4. Deposit deductions are limited to unpaid rent, damage beyond normal wear and tear, unpaid utilities/charges, and abandoned-property removal.
5. Burlington and Barre deposits are reviewed under local law.
6. Repair complaints are tracked in writing and addressed promptly.
7. Entry notices provide at least 48 hours and entry occurs between 9 a.m. and 9 p.m.
8. Eviction notices match the statutory reason and notice period.
9. No lockouts, utility shutoffs, or property removal occur without proper judicial process.
10. Fair housing, voucher/public assistance, disability accommodation, and domestic-violence survivor protections are built into screening and operations.

## Gap Analysis

This report covers statewide Vermont law and selected Burlington/Barre local issues. It does not fully audit every municipal housing code, local rental registry, town health officer enforcement practice, short-term rental rule, subsidized housing rule, mobile home park rule, or unpublished trial-court practice.

For a property-specific decision, the key missing facts are city/town, lease type, subsidy status, tenant length of occupancy, whether the unit is in Burlington or Barre, written notices served, repair history, inspection records, deposit language, and any fair housing accommodation requests.
