# New Hampshire Tenant Rights Report

**Scope:** New Hampshire residential tenant rights, current as of **July 28, 2026**. This is an informational compliance report, not legal advice.

## Executive Summary

New Hampshire tenant protections are concentrated in **RSA 540**, **RSA 540-A**, **RSA 48-A**, **RSA 354-A**, **RSA 130-A**, and **RSA 205-A**. The most important rights are: no self-help eviction, written eviction notice, court process before removal, protection against retaliatory eviction, safe and habitable housing, limits on security deposits, fair-housing protections, and special protections for survivors of domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking.

Two recent changes matter immediately. Effective **January 1, 2026**, landlords may not require residential tenants or applicants to pay lease amounts solely by electronic funds transfer and must allow at least one non-electronic payment method. ([gc.nh.gov](https://gc.nh.gov/bill_status/legacy/bs2016/billText.aspx?id=484&sy=2025&txtFormat=html)) Effective **July 1, 2026**, expiration of a long-term lease can be a ground for termination if statutory conditions are met, but that “no-fault termination” must not be treated as an eviction for rental applications and tenant screening reports. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://gencourt.state.nh.us/bill_status/legacy/bs2016/billText.aspx?id=38&sy=2025&txtFormat=html))

## 1. Who Is Covered

New Hampshire treats every tenancy as at-will unless a different contract is shown. RSA 540 defines “restricted property” as most residential rental property except certain small-owner or owner-occupied categories. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/lv/540/540-mrg.htm))

That distinction matters because restricted residential property may be terminated only for statutory reasons, such as nonpayment, substantial damage, material lease breach, health/safety behavior, other good cause, certain lead-abatement situations, bed-bug preparation failure, domestic-violence-related household removal, and, as of July 1, 2026, certain lease-expiration cases. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/lv/540/540-mrg.htm))

RSA 540 excludes some occupancies, including transient rooming-house stays under 90 consecutive days, vacation/recreational lodging, student dormitories, licensed care facilities, certain owner-occupied single-family arrangements, vacation rentals, and certain caregiver housing. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/lv/540/540-mrg.htm))

## 2. Right To Safe, Sanitary, Habitable Housing

New Hampshire law requires minimum housing standards. **RSA 48-A:14** covers rental property in municipalities without their own housing codes and identifies conditions landlords may not maintain, including infestation, defective plumbing, dangerous electrical conditions, consistent roof or wall leaks, falling plaster, serious holes in floors/walls/ceilings, unsafe porches/stairs/railings, garbage in common areas, inadequate water or hot-water equipment, gas leaks, and inadequate heat. ([gc.nh.gov](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/48-A/48-A-14.htm))

Heat is specifically protected: heating facilities must be properly installed, safely maintained, in good working condition, and capable of safely and adequately heating habitable rooms, bathrooms, and toilet rooms to an average of at least **65 degrees Fahrenheit**; if heat is included in rent, the premises must actually be maintained at that minimum average temperature. ([gc.nh.gov](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/48-A/48-A-14.htm))

Local housing codes may impose higher standards. RSA 48-A provides a state floor, not necessarily the whole local rulebook. The New Hampshire court library notes RSA 48-A’s purpose is housing standards and repair authority. ([courts-state-nh-us.libguides.com](https://courts-state-nh-us.libguides.com/c.php?g=889061&p=6391294))

## 3. Repairs, Code Problems, And Rent-Related Defenses

A tenant facing a nonpayment eviction may raise a habitability defense if the premises substantially violate RSA 48-A or local housing codes, the violation materially affects habitability, the tenant gave written notice while not in arrears, the landlord failed to correct within 14 days or as promptly as emergencies require, the tenant did not cause the problem, and the tenant did not block reasonable access. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/lv/540/540-mrg.htm))

If that defense is raised, the court may continue the case for up to one month and require withheld rent to be paid into court. If repairs are made, the court may apportion rent between the landlord and tenant based on fair rental value while defective; if repairs are not made, judgment may enter for the tenant and deposited money may be returned. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/lv/540/540-mrg.htm))

## 4. Bed Bugs, Rodents, And Infestations

Landlords may not willfully fail to investigate a tenant’s report of insects, bed bugs, or rodents within **7 days** of receiving notice from the tenant or a municipal health or housing code authority, and must take reasonable remediation measures. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/LV/540-A/540-A-mrg.htm))

Landlords generally bear reasonable bed-bug remediation costs, but may recover costs if they prove the tenant was responsible and offered a reasonable repayment agreement. Failure to pay after remediation can become a nonpayment eviction ground only under the statutory conditions. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/lv/540/540-mrg.htm))

Tenants must allow lawful access for emergency repairs and, after 48 hours’ written notice, inspection for bed bugs when an adjacent, above, or below unit has reported bed bugs. Tenants must also comply with reasonable written preparation instructions at least 72 hours before remediation. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/LV/540-A/540-A-mrg.htm))

## 5. Quiet Enjoyment And No Self-Help Eviction

A landlord may not willfully violate a tenant’s quiet enjoyment or bypass lawful eviction procedures. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/LV/540-A/540-A-mrg.htm))

Specific prohibited acts include willfully interrupting utilities such as water, heat, electricity, gas, sewerage, elevator, refrigeration, or telephone service, except temporary interruptions needed for actual repairs or emergencies. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/LV/540-A/540-A-mrg.htm))

A landlord may not deny access to the rented premises except through proper judicial process, and may not seize or deny access to a tenant’s property except through proper judicial process. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/LV/540-A/540-A-mrg.htm))

## 6. Landlord Entry And Tenant Privacy

A landlord may not willfully enter a tenant’s premises without prior consent except for emergency repairs. For non-consensual entry, the landlord generally needs either an emergency-repair basis or a court order. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/LV/540-A/540-A-mrg.htm))

Tenants may not willfully refuse landlord access for necessary repairs or other reasonable and lawful ownership functions at a reasonable time after notice adequate under the circumstances. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/LV/540-A/540-A-mrg.htm))

## 7. Application Fees And Rent Payment Methods

Before collecting any application or renewal fee, a landlord must clearly disclose the fee amount in writing and disclose any criminal-background or credit-check requirement. If the applicant does not rent the unit, the landlord must return any amount beyond documented background-check, credit-check, and reasonable administrative costs within 30 days. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/LV/540-A/540-A-mrg.htm))

As of **January 1, 2026**, a landlord may not require a residential tenant or prospective tenant to pay amounts due under a lease, renewal, or extension solely through electronic funds transfer, including automatic recurring electronic transfers. The landlord must allow at least one non-electronic payment method. ([gc.nh.gov](https://gc.nh.gov/bill_status/legacy/bs2016/billText.aspx?id=484&sy=2025&txtFormat=html))

## 8. Security Deposits

For covered landlords, a security deposit may not exceed **one month’s rent or $100, whichever is greater**. “Security deposit” means funds in excess of monthly rent transferred from the tenant for any purpose. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/LV/540-A/540-A-mrg.htm))

Important exception: for the security-deposit subdivision, a person renting a single-family residence while owning no other rental property, or renting units in an owner-occupied building of 5 units or fewer, is generally not a “landlord” unless the unit is occupied by someone age 60 or older. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/LV/540-A/540-A-mrg.htm))

On receiving a deposit, the landlord generally must provide a signed receipt stating the amount and where the deposit or bond will be held, and must notify the tenant that conditions needing repair should be noted on the receipt or provided in writing within **5 days** of occupancy. A receipt is not required for certain check or agency payments, but the written 5-day condition notice is still required. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/LV/540-A/540-A-mrg.htm))

Security deposits remain the tenant’s money and must be held in trust, not mixed with the landlord’s personal funds, subject to statutory trust-account rules. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/LV/540-A/540-A-mrg.htm))

If the landlord holds the deposit for at least one year, the tenant is owed interest at the rate paid on regular savings accounts at the New Hampshire bank, savings and loan, or credit union where deposited. Tenants may request deposit-location and interest information, and may inspect deposit records. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/LV/540-A/540-A-mrg.htm))

The landlord must return the security deposit and interest, if any, within **30 days** after the tenancy ends, unless lawful deductions apply. Deductions for damage must exclude reasonable wear and tear and must be supported by a written itemized list and satisfactory evidence such as receipts, estimates, bills, or invoices. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/LV/540-A/540-A-mrg.htm))

If the landlord fails to comply with return, interest, or accounting requirements, the tenant may recover damages equal to twice the deposit plus interest owed, minus lawful charges. Lease provisions waiving these security-deposit rights are void. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/LV/540-A/540-A-mrg.htm))

## 9. Eviction Rights And Procedure

Residential eviction generally requires written notice and court process. A residential eviction notice must state the reason with specificity. For residential tenancies, 30 days’ notice is generally sufficient, but 7 days is sufficient for nonpayment, substantial damage, health/safety behavior, or certain domestic-violence-related household-member cases. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/lv/540/540-mrg.htm))

For nonpayment, the demand for rent may be served after rent is due and before or at the same time as the eviction notice. A landlord may not demand more than the whole rent in arrears. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/lv/540/540-mrg.htm))

A tenant in a nonpayment case may avoid dismissal if, before the merits hearing, the tenant pays all rent due through the payment date plus lawful lease charges, $15 liquidated damages, and landlord filing and service costs, subject to the statutory limit that this cure cannot be used more than 3 times in a 12-month period. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/lv/540/540-mrg.htm))

Rental-assistance vouchers or written promises to pay by state, county, municipal, or qualifying organizations count as payment for the represented amount; refusal of timely tender or failure to provide ordinary verification needed for assistance can be an affirmative defense. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/lv/540/540-mrg.htm))

A tenant who wants to contest an eviction must file an appearance by the return day on the writ. The tenant cannot be removed unless the court orders eviction, although failure to appear can result in an order. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/lv/540/540-mrg.htm))

If the landlord claims unpaid rent in the eviction case, the court must consider tenant defenses, claims, or counterclaims that offset or reduce rent owed. If tenant setoffs or counterclaims equal or exceed the claimed rent, judgment in the possessory action is granted for the tenant. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/lv/540/540-mrg.htm))

## 10. Retaliation Protections

Except where the tenant owes at least one week’s rent, retaliation is a defense to possession if the eviction is in retaliation for reporting code or RSA 540-A violations, initiating a good-faith RSA 540-A action or habitability procedure, or meeting/gathering with other tenants for lawful purposes. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/lv/540/540-mrg.htm))

A rebuttable presumption of retaliation arises when eviction, rent increase, or substantial tenancy changes occur within 6 months after protected tenant activity or landlord notice of a complaint, subject to statutory details. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/lv/540/540-mrg.htm))

If retaliation is successfully raised, the court may award tenant damages of up to 3 months’ rent. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/lv/540/540-mrg.htm))

## 11. Domestic Violence, Sexual Assault, And Stalking Protections

A landlord of restricted property may not terminate tenancy solely because a tenant or household member is a victim of domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking if the tenant provides statutory verification, including a protective order, law-enforcement report, professional verification, or circuit-court self-certification form. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/lv/540/540-mrg.htm))

A tenant with a protective order granting possession of the dwelling may request a lock change or rekey at the tenant’s expense; the landlord must comply after receiving the order and may not give new keys to the restrained or excluded person. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/lv/540/540-mrg.htm))

Verification that someone is a victim must be kept strictly confidential, except for written consent, relevant use in an eviction or housing-assistance termination proceeding, or other legal requirement. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/lv/540/540-mrg.htm))

## 12. Fair Housing Rights

New Hampshire recognizes equal housing opportunity as a civil right. Protected characteristics include age, sex, gender identity, race, creed, color, marital status, familial status, physical or mental disability, national origin, and sexual orientation. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/xxxi/354-a/354-a-mrg.htm))

RSA 354-A prohibits discriminatory real estate practices including refusing to rent, altering terms or services, refusing to negotiate, falsely representing availability, discriminatory advertising, and certain discriminatory listing practices. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/xxxi/354-a/354-a-mrg.htm))

Disability protections include reasonable modifications at the tenant’s expense when necessary for full enjoyment, reasonable accommodations in rules, policies, practices, or services, and accessible design requirements for covered multifamily dwellings. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/xxxi/354-a/354-a-mrg.htm))

Retaliation for opposing discrimination or filing, testifying, or assisting in a discrimination proceeding is itself unlawful. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/xxxi/354-a/354-a-mrg.htm))

## 13. Lead Paint And Lead Hazards

If lead-hazard reduction forces relocation, New Hampshire law gives tenants rights around relocation costs, comparable replacement housing, return to the original unit, return of security deposit and prepaid rent before vacating, and written notice of reoccupancy rights. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/X/130-A/130-A-8-a.htm))

If an owner withdraws a unit from the rental market instead of reducing lead hazards, the unit may not later be rented for residential use without reducing the lead hazards; violations can create statutory liability. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/X/130-A/130-A-8-a.htm))

If a child tests positive for lead above statutory standards, DHHS must test the unit’s water for lead; if above the EPA action level, the landlord must notify the tenant or prospective tenant and install a certified filtering device unless the lead source is removed and water tests below the action level. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/LV/540-A/540-A-mrg.htm))

## 14. Manufactured Housing Park Tenants

Manufactured-housing park tenants have additional protections under **RSA 205-A**. RSA 540 applies where not inconsistent with manufactured-housing park law. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/xvii/205-a/205-a-mrg.htm))

Park owners must use statutory termination reasons and notice periods; the search result summary identifies 30-day, 60-day, and 18-month notice periods depending on the ground, plus a requirement that termination notices specify the reason. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/xvii/205-a/205-a-mrg.htm))

Manufactured-housing leases cannot waive rights under RSA 205-A or RSA 540-A; any such waiver is unenforceable and void. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/xvii/205-a/205-a-mrg.htm))

## 15. Personal Property After Moving Or Eviction

Except for certain excluded categories, a landlord must exercise reasonable care in storing personal property of a tenant who vacated voluntarily or by eviction for **7 days** after the tenant vacates. During that period, the tenant may recover property without paying rent or storage fees. After 7 days, the landlord may dispose of the property without notice. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/LV/540-A/540-A-mrg.htm))

Abandonment rules are detailed and require notice in many circumstances. A statutory abandonment notice must identify specific reasons and inform tenants of their right to retrieve property and file a 540-A petition. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/LV/540-A/540-A-mrg.htm))

## 16. Current Watch List

A 2026 bill proposal would require written leases to expressly assign snow and ice removal duties, place common-area snow/ice responsibility on the landlord unless otherwise assigned, and apply to leases entered or renewed after January 1, 2027. As located, this is a bill text/watch-list item, not cited here as enacted law. ([gencourt.state.nh.us](https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/lsr_search/billText.aspx?id=1836&type=4))

House Bill 1336 was reported by New Hampshire REALTORS as creating a proposed “regulated conditional deposit” category that could allow an extra month’s rent in limited applicant-screening situations. Treat this as a watch-list item unless confirmed enacted in the General Court’s final law records. ([nhar.org](https://nhar.org/news/article/state-house-may-19-2026))

## Gap Analysis

This report covers statewide New Hampshire law and selected official sources. It does not fully audit every municipal housing, rental inspection, certificate, health-officer, fire-code, or zoning ordinance. Local ordinances can materially change tenant rights and landlord duties, especially in larger municipalities or towns with adopted housing codes.

For an individual dispute, the key missing facts are lease terms, property type, city/town, whether the property is restricted or nonrestricted, payment history, notices served, code-inspection records, communications, and whether federal housing subsidies or fair-housing accommodations are involved.
