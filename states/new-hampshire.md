# Executive Brief: New Hampshire Real-Estate Compliance

**Scope:** Recent and upcoming landlord/tenant, lease-compliance, eviction, housing, and property-management developments through **July 27, 2026**. Informational only, not legal advice.

### 1. Lease expiration is now an express eviction ground

Effective **July 1, 2026**, expiration of a lease can support a possessory action when the original lease was at least 12 months, or successive shorter terms have continued for at least 12 months. The landlord must give at least **60 days’ written nonrenewal notice** and file the possessory action within **six months after expiration**. Retaliation and discrimination defenses remain available. ([New Hampshire Governor's Council][1])

**Operational action:** Add automated 60-day nonrenewal deadlines and preserve proof of service. Do not assume lease expiration alone permits physical removal without a court order.

### 2. Electronic rent payment cannot be the only option

Since **January 1, 2026**, landlords may offer portals, ACH, Venmo, or recurring electronic payments, but cannot require tenants or applicants to use electronic funds transfer as the sole payment method. At least one **non-electronic payment option** must remain available. ([New Hampshire Governor's Council][2])

**Operational action:** Review leases, renewal documents, resident portals, convenience-fee language, and payment instructions. Clearly identify an accepted non-electronic method and document payments consistently.

### 3. Major eviction-procedure changes begin October 8, 2026

HB 1598, Chapter 308, takes effect **October 8, 2026** and materially revises eviction litigation. Important changes include:

* Tenants contesting an eviction must file an appearance by the return date and an answer within five days afterward.
* Landlords may include an unpaid-rent claim in the eviction case.
* The court may enter a related money judgment, generally capped at **$1,500** in that proceeding, without preventing later litigation for additional amounts.
* Both parties receive discovery rights tied to claims and defenses.
* A landlord may obtain a short continuance when a tenant raises an undisclosed defense at the hearing.
* Parties generally must exchange intended documentary evidence at least five days before certain RSA 540-A hearings.
* Tenant conduct that willfully threatens health or safety, prevents emergency repairs, or unreasonably interferes with others receives clearer statutory treatment.
* Tenants must permit reasonable access for repairs and other lawful ownership functions after adequate notice.

**Operational action:** Replace legacy eviction checklists before October. Coordinate notices, pleadings, rent ledgers, inspection records, communications, and evidence-exchange deadlines with counsel and the latest Judicial Branch forms.

### 4. Court forms and notices require active version control

The New Hampshire Judicial Branch reports that new eviction notices and demands for rent have been issued in response to statutory changes. Its landlord/tenant page directs users to RSA 540 and District Court Rules 5.1–5.12, while its forms library contains specialized forms for manufactured housing, unauthorized occupants, payment-based dismissals, domestic-violence protections, and RSA 540-A petitions. ([New Hampshire Judicial Branch][3])

**Operational action:** Remove saved local copies of old forms from shared drives and require staff to retrieve the current version from the court website before service or filing.

### 5. Rent increases still require careful notice handling

Current RSA 540 permits a tenant’s refusal to accept a rent increase to constitute good cause for eviction only when the landlord gave written notice stating the new amount and effective date at least **30 days before the increase**. A rent increase, substantial tenancy change, or possessory action within six months of certain tenant complaints or protected activity may also create a rebuttable presumption of retaliation. ([New Hampshire Governor's Council][1])

**Operational action:** Separate ordinary rent-increase documentation from eviction decision-making. Conduct a retaliation review whenever a tenant recently reported code, safety, habitability, or RSA 540-A concerns.

### 6. Homeowners’ association disclosures change January 1, 2027

HB 1523, Chapter 306, was signed July 10, 2026 and becomes effective **January 1, 2027**. Its final title concerns disclosure requirements for homeowners’ associations. ([New Hampshire Governor's Council][4])

**Operational action:** Condominium and HOA managers should review resale, owner, governance, fee, and association-document disclosure workflows once the final statutory language and any implementation guidance are incorporated into standard closing packages.

### 7. Legislative watch: residential application fees

HB 1371 was introduced in 2026 to prohibit residential rental application fees and prevent landlords from rejecting applications solely because an applicant lacks a Social Security number, provided another permitted identifier is supplied. The material reviewed shows the proposal as introduced, not enacted. ([New Hampshire Governor's Council][5])

**Watch action:** Do not treat this as current law, but track it or successor legislation. Application-fee and screening practices remain a likely compliance target.

## Immediate management priorities

1. Confirm every property offers a non-electronic payment method.
2. Add the new 60-day lease-expiration/nonrenewal workflow.
3. Rebuild eviction procedures before **October 8, 2026**.
4. Download current court forms rather than reusing stored templates.
5. Audit rent increases for notice sufficiency and retaliation risk.
6. Prepare HOA disclosure packages for the **January 1, 2027** change.
