# Maine Tenant Rights Report

**Scope:** Maine residential renter rights and landlord compliance duties, current as of **July 29, 2026**. This is an informational compliance report, not legal advice.

## Executive Summary

Maine renter protections are concentrated in **Title 14, Chapters 709, 710, and 710-A**, plus the Maine Human Rights Act and selected municipal rules. The highest-risk compliance areas for landlords and property managers are: fee transparency, application-fee restrictions, rent-increase notices, security deposit limits and return deadlines, habitability, bedbugs, radon, energy-efficiency disclosures, no self-help eviction, anti-retaliation, fair housing, and Portland’s rent-control/rental-registration system.

The newest operational issue is Maine’s rental **total price disclosure** framework. Landlords must disclose rent, mandatory recurring fees, optional recurring fees, utility service costs, and other tenant-paid costs before entering a lease or tenancy-at-will agreement, in plain language and signed by both parties. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6030-J.html)) Maine also now limits what landlords can demand at move-in to the first full month’s rent, lawful security deposit, and properly disclosed mandatory recurring fees. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6022-A.html))

## 1. High Severity / Statutory Risk

### Total Price Disclosure Statement

Maine landlords must give prospective tenants a written disclosure before the lease or tenancy-at-will agreement is entered. The disclosure must include total rent, mandatory recurring fees, optional recurring fees, utility service costs, and other tenant-paid costs. It must be plain, understandable, signed by both parties, and copied to both parties. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6030-J.html))

Practical action: use a Maine-specific signed fee and cost disclosure for every new tenancy, especially where tenants pay utilities, parking, trash, pet fees, administrative fees, or other recurring charges.

### Application Fees Restricted

Maine generally prohibits landlords from charging application, review, or approval fees. A landlord may charge only the actual cost of **one** background check, credit check, or other screening process, must provide the applicant a complete copy of the screening information, and may not charge more than one such fee in a 12-month period. ([legislature.maine.gov](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/14/title14sec6030-H.html))

Practical action: remove flat application fees. Keep receipts or invoices showing actual screening costs.

### Rent and Mandatory Recurring Fee Increase Notices

For residential estates, rent or mandatory recurring fees may be increased only after at least **45 days’ written notice**. If rent is increased by **10% or more**, or multiple increases within 12 months add up to 10% or more, the landlord must give at least **75 days’ written notice**. Waivers are void, and violations can require return of unlawfully obtained sums, interest, attorney’s fees, and costs. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6015-2.html))

Practical action: track cumulative rent increases over rolling 12-month periods.

### Initial Move-In Payment Cap

At the start of a lease or tenancy-at-will agreement, a landlord, agent, or broker may not require more than the first full month’s rent, the security deposit permitted by law, and any properly disclosed mandatory recurring fee. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6022-A.html))

Practical action: avoid collecting extra “holding,” “administrative,” “amenity,” or prepaid charges unless clearly permitted and disclosed.

### Security Deposit Exposure

Maine caps residential security deposits at **two months’ rent**. ([legislature.maine.gov](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/14/title14sec6032.html)) The landlord must return the deposit or provide an itemized written retention statement within the lease deadline, not more than **30 days**, or within **21 days** for tenancy-at-will after termination or surrender and acceptance, whichever is later. Normal wear and tear cannot be charged to the deposit. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6033.html)) Wrongful retention can expose the landlord to double damages, attorney’s fees, and costs. ([codes.findlaw.com](https://codes.findlaw.com/me/title-14-court-procedure-civil/me-rev-st-tit-14-sect-6034/))

Practical action: document move-in/move-out condition, itemize deductions, and calendar return deadlines.

## 2. Habitability, Repairs, Heat, And Utilities

Maine recognizes an implied warranty and covenant of habitability. A tenant may bring a court complaint if a condition renders the unit unfit for human habitation, endangers or materially impairs health or safety, was not caused by the tenant, was reported to the landlord, and the landlord unreasonably failed to take prompt, effective repair steps. Courts may order repairs, adjust rent value, authorize temporary vacancy, or issue protective orders. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6021.html))

Common habitability issues include unsafe or insufficient heat, undrinkable water, insects or rodents, unsafe heating systems, leaking ceilings, broken windows or exterior doors that cannot lock, missing smoke or carbon monoxide detectors, loss of electricity, running water, or hot water. Pine Tree Legal Assistance summarizes Maine’s practical habitability floor and notes living spaces should be heatable to at least **68 degrees**. ([ptla.org](https://www.ptla.org/rights-maine-renters-unsafe-or-unfit-housing))

Maine also has a repair-and-deduct remedy for dangerous minor conditions where the reasonable cost is less than **$500** or half the monthly rent, whichever is greater, subject to notice and statutory conditions. ([legislature.maine.gov](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/14/title14sec6026.pdf))

For common-area heat and utilities, tenants generally cannot be made to pay common-area utility costs unless the arrangement complies with Maine law. Violations can produce actual damages or **$250**, whichever is greater, plus attorney’s fees and costs. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6024.html)) If the landlord fails to pay a landlord-account utility bill, the tenant may be able to pay and deduct under statutory rules. ([codes.findlaw.com](https://codes.findlaw.com/me/title-14-court-procedure-civil/me-rev-st-tit-14-sect-6024-a/))

## 3. Bedbugs And Pest Issues

Maine has specific bedbug rules. After written or oral tenant notice that a unit may have bedbugs, the landlord must inspect within **5 days**. If infestation exists, the landlord must contact a pest control agent within **10 days**, take reasonable treatment measures, and use an insured pest control agent. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/Statutes/14/title14sec6021-A.html))

Landlords may not rent a unit they know or suspect is infested with bedbugs, and must disclose adjacent-unit infestations or treatment before renting. Tenants must promptly notify landlords of suspected infestation, allow reasonable access, and comply with reasonable control measures. Landlord noncompliance can produce a **$250** penalty or actual damages, whichever is greater, plus attorney’s fees. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/Statutes/14/title14sec6021-A.html))

## 4. Radon, Energy, Smoking, And Required Disclosures

Maine requires radon testing in residential rental buildings and notice to tenants. Existing rental buildings had to be tested by March 1, 2014, and every 10 years thereafter when requested by a tenant unless mitigation has been installed. New residential buildings must be tested within 12 months of tenant occupancy. Landlords must provide written notice of test results before lease/payment or within 30 days for existing tenants. Violations can carry civil penalties, and failure to provide notice or falsification is treated as a breach of habitability. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6030-D.html))

If radon testing shows **4.0 pCi/L or more**, either landlord or tenant may terminate the lease or tenancy-at-will with at least 30 days’ notice, and the landlord generally may not keep the deposit due to that radon-based termination except as otherwise allowed for lawful deductions. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6030-D.html))

Maine energy-efficiency disclosure rules give prospective tenants who will pay utility costs the right to obtain 12 months of energy consumption and cost information from the supplier. Landlords must provide a residential energy-efficiency disclosure statement to prospective tenants who pay energy supply costs, or upon tenant request, before contract/deposit, obtain signatures, and retain the signed statement for at least 3 years. Beginning **January 1, 2030**, a tenant who did not receive the signed statement may terminate with 30 days’ written notice. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6030-C.html))

Landlords must also provide written notice of the smoking policy before lease or deposit, stating whether smoking is prohibited, allowed throughout the premises, or allowed only in identified areas. MaineHousing summarizes this landlord duty alongside energy and detector obligations. ([mainehousing.org](https://www.mainehousing.org/programs-services/rental/landlords))

## 5. Landlord Entry And Tenant Privacy

Maine landlords may enter without consent only in limited circumstances. Except for emergencies or impracticability, the landlord must give reasonable notice and enter only at reasonable times. **Twenty-four hours** is presumed reasonable unless evidence shows otherwise. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6025.html))

Practical action: give written 24-hour notice for inspections, repairs, showings, and pest-control access unless a true emergency exists.

## 6. Late Fees And Rent Payments

A Maine rent payment is not late until it is unpaid for **15 days** after the due date. A late fee may not exceed **4%** of the amount due for one month, and the landlord cannot assess a late fee unless the tenant received written notice at the time the rental agreement was entered. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6028.html))

Practical action: leases should state the late-fee amount clearly and cap it at 4% of one month’s rent. Daily or compounding late fees are high-risk.

## 7. Eviction Rights And No Self-Help Eviction

Maine landlords may not remove tenants through self-help. Illegal evictions include utility shutoffs, lockouts, denial of access to the premises, or seizure/denial of access to the tenant’s property without proper judicial process. Remedies include actual damages or **$250**, whichever is greater, plus costs and attorney’s fees. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6014.html))

For tenancies at will, Maine generally requires at least **30 days’ written notice** to terminate, subject to statutory exceptions. The notice must expire on or after the date through which rent has been paid if the tenant has already paid rent through the 30-day period. ([legislature.maine.gov](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/14/title14sec6002.html))

For nonpayment, the Maine Judicial Branch explains that a 7-day notice to quit may be used when the tenant is at least 7 days late, and the notice is void if the tenant pays the amount due within the 7-day period. ([courts.maine.gov](https://www.courts.maine.gov/help/eviction/evict-tenant.html))

Beginning January 1, 2024, landlords must attach the Judicial Branch form notice to a notice to terminate tenancy served on a tenant. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6001.html))

## 8. Retaliation Protections

Maine creates retaliation protections when tenants assert protected rights before being served with an eviction notice. Protected activity includes good-faith fair housing complaints, notice that the tenant or minor child is a victim of domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking, and complaints about landlord sexual harassment, among other protected acts. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6001.html))

Bedbug-related assertions also trigger a rebuttable presumption of retaliation if an eviction action is filed within 6 months after the tenant asserted rights under the bedbug statute, subject to exceptions for nonpayment or substantial damage. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/Statutes/14/title14sec6021-A.html))

## 9. Domestic Violence, Sexual Assault, Stalking, And Sexual Harassment

A tenant who is a victim of domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking may not be evicted based on incidents of actual or threatened abuse occurring at the premises or reports of such incidents where those incidents are framed as nuisance, property damage, or lease violations. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6001.html))

A victim may terminate a lease early with **7 days’ written notice** for a lease under one year, or **30 days’ written notice** for a lease of one year or more, plus required documentation. Maine also permits lease bifurcation to remove a perpetrator while protecting the victim’s property rights under valid court orders. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6001.html))

A tenant who is the victim of sexual harassment by the landlord or the landlord’s agent may also terminate under the same framework with documentation. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6001.html))

## 10. Fair Housing And Discrimination

The Maine Human Rights Act prohibits unlawful housing discrimination. Protected categories include race, color, sex, sexual orientation or gender identity, physical or mental disability, religion, ancestry, national origin, familial status, and certain protection-order history. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/5/title5sec4581-A.html)) Pine Tree Legal Assistance also summarizes Maine renter protections against discrimination based on receipt of public assistance, disability, familial status, sexual orientation, gender identity/expression, and other protected traits. ([ptla.org](https://www.ptla.org/rights-maine-renters-discrimination))

Important case-law caution: in ***Dussault v. RRE Coach Lantern Holdings, LLC***, the Maine Supreme Judicial Court held that a landlord did not violate the Maine Human Rights Act by refusing to include a Section 8 tenancy addendum in its standard lease under the facts presented. That case remains a major limitation/watch point in voucher-related rental discrimination analysis. ([law.justia.com](https://law.justia.com/cases/maine/supreme-court/2014/2014-me-8.html))

## 11. Portland And Local Compliance

Maine does not have statewide rent control, but **Portland** has a major local rent-control and rental-registration system. Portland’s Rent Control Ordinance took effect January 1, 2021, establishes base rent for most rental units, and caps annual increases. ([portlandmaine.gov](https://www.portlandmaine.gov/rent-control-rental-housing-rights)) Portland announced the allowable increase percentage for calendar year **2026** as **2.2%**. ([portlandmaine.gov](https://www.portlandmaine.gov/city-news?contentId=bcef8112-7244-44fe-89ea-aa0a872aa2c0))

Portland rental properties must also be registered with the city; Portland’s long-term rental registration page notes that as of **July 1, 2026**, the long-term rental registration fee increased to **$55 per unit annually**. ([portlandmaine.gov](https://www.portlandmaine.gov/long-term-rental-registration))

Practical action: treat Portland properties separately from the Maine statewide baseline. Portland rent notices, registration, rent-board issues, and rent-increase calculations need local review.

## 12. Watch List / Background Items

**Source-of-income and voucher discrimination:** LD 847 was introduced in the 132nd Legislature as a source-of-income/housing discrimination bill but was changed into a resolve to study tenant-landlord relationships and voucher use. Tracking sources show the discrimination version did not become a broad enacted source-of-income ban as originally proposed. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/bills/display_ps.asp?PID=1456&ld=847&snum=132)) ([fastdemocracy.com](https://fastdemocracy.com/bill-search/me/132/bills/MEB00014152/))

**Rental registry:** LD 1806 proposed a statewide residential rental unit registry and would have tied some landlord eviction rights to registration, but LegiScan reports it failed on March 12, 2026. ([legiscan.com](https://legiscan.com/ME/bill/LD1806/2025))

**Energy disclosure:** The 2030 energy-disclosure lease-termination remedy is not immediate, but it is a real future compliance deadline. Lease packets should be updated before then. ([mainelegislature.org](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6030-C.html))

## 13. Practical Compliance Checklist

For Maine rental operations, confirm that each file includes:

1. Signed total price disclosure.
2. Application-fee documentation showing actual screening cost only.
3. Lease or addendum disclosing lawful late fee.
4. Security deposit amount at or below two months’ rent.
5. Move-in condition record and deposit-account documentation.
6. Energy-efficiency disclosure and 12-month energy-cost information path.
7. Radon disclosure and test records.
8. Smoking policy notice.
9. Lead-paint disclosures and renovation notices where applicable.
10. Bedbug disclosure and response workflow.
11. Rent-increase calendar using 45-day and 75-day rules.
12. Judicial Branch notice attachment for termination notices.
13. Portland-specific registration and rent-control tracking for Portland properties.

## Gap Analysis

This report covers statewide Maine law and key Portland local compliance. It does not exhaustively audit every municipal housing, code-enforcement, short-term rental, rental-registration, fire-code, or local health-board requirement. Local rules can matter in Portland, South Portland, Bangor, Lewiston, Biddeford, Bar Harbor, and coastal/tourism-heavy communities.

For a property-specific legal decision, the missing facts are: city/town, lease type, subsidy status, building size and owner-occupancy, whether the unit is in Portland or another rent-regulated municipality, fee schedule, utility arrangement, radon/energy records, notices served, and whether the tenant has asserted protected rights.

## Renters Insurance Tips For 2026

Informational shopping guidance for Maine renters. Availability, pricing, and coverage vary by ZIP code and underwriting; confirm details with licensed insurers and the [Maine Bureau of Insurance](https://www.maine.gov/pfr/insurance/).

### Best renters insurance shortlist for 2026

1. **State Farm** — Best broad default choice for many renters because it is widely available and often inexpensive. Forbes named State Farm one of its top renters insurance picks for 2026, and NerdWallet also highlights it as a strong affordable option.
2. **Amica** — Best for customer service. NerdWallet names Amica among its top 2026 renters insurance companies, especially for renters who value service.
3. **USAA** — Best if eligible. It is limited to military members, veterans, some federal workers, and qualifying families, but NerdWallet notes rare built-in flood and earthquake coverage.
4. **Lemonade** — Worth quoting for young renters who want a fast app-based experience, though availability, claims experience, and pricing vary by state.
5. **Travelers, Nationwide, Allstate, Progressive, or Liberty Mutual** — Good quote-comparison options, especially if bundling with auto insurance.

### What to tell brand-new renters

Renters insurance usually covers three big things: personal property, personal liability, and additional living expenses if the apartment becomes unlivable after a covered loss. The NAIC gives the same basic framework: property coverage plus liability are core parts of most renters policies.

**Recommended starter coverage:**

* **Personal property:** usually $20,000–$30,000 minimum for a first apartment; more if they have expensive electronics, bikes, instruments, tools, or furniture.
* **Liability:** choose at least $100,000; $300,000 is often a small price increase and better protection.
* **Deductible:** $500 is a common middle ground.
* **Replacement cost coverage:** strongly prefer this over actual cash value. Replacement cost pays closer to the cost of buying new items; actual cash value subtracts depreciation.
* **Add-ons to ask about:** scheduled coverage for jewelry/electronics/bikes, water backup, pet liability, identity theft, and coverage for roommates only if actually listed.

### Simple buying script

> Get quotes from State Farm, Amica, Lemonade, and whoever handles your car insurance. Use the same limits for each quote: $30,000 personal property, $300,000 liability, $500 deductible, replacement cost coverage. Pick the best mix of price, coverage, and claims reputation.

### Important warnings

Renters insurance usually does **not** cover the building itself, a roommate’s belongings, floods, earthquakes, pest damage, normal wear and tear, or business equipment above small limits unless added. If they live in a basement, coastal area, flood-prone area, or older building, they should ask specifically about water, sewer backup, and flood coverage.
