# Rhode Island Tenant Rights Report

**Scope:** Current as of September 13, 2026. Primary statute: R.I. Gen. Laws Title 34, Chapter 18. Official handbook: RI Executive Office of Housing Landlord Tenant Handbook.

## Executive Summary

Rhode Island is a uniform statewide landlord-tenant jurisdiction. Municipalities generally may not enact local rent control (s. 34-18-7). Core rights: security deposit capped at one month of rent; 20-day deposit return with itemization; 60-day written rent-increase notice (120 days for month-to-month tenants over age 62); court process before lockout; up to 3x rent or 3x damages for illegal lockout or utility shutoff; habitability under s. 34-18-22.

## 1. High Severity / Statutory Risk

### Security deposits

A landlord may not demand a security deposit in excess of one month of periodic rent (s. 34-18-19(a)). Return within 20 days after the later of termination, delivery of possession, or forwarding address, with a written itemized notice. Lawful deductions: unpaid accrued rent, reasonable cleaning, reasonable trash disposal, and physical damage other than ordinary wear and tear. Missed compliance: amount due plus twice the amount wrongfully withheld, plus reasonable attorney fees (s. 34-18-19(c)). No statewide escrow or interest requirement. Separate furniture deposits follow a parallel 20-day rule.

### Rent-increase notice

Written notice at least 60 days before the effective date. Month-to-month tenants over age 62: at least 120 days. No statewide percentage cap. (s. 34-18-16.1, effective June 24, 2024.) Housing-program rules can shorten the window if a federal or state program requires it.

### Application / screening

Chapter 34-18 does not impose a Vermont-style statewide application-fee ban. Confirm the current handbook and session law before charging processing fees.

## 2. Habitability And Repairs

Landlord duties include code compliance affecting health and safety, repairs for habitability, safe common areas, running water, reasonable hot water, and reasonable heat except where the building is not required to be equipped or the tenant controls the utility (s. 34-18-22). Tenant remedies after notice include the paths in ss. 34-18-28 through 34-18-32. Get advice before withholding rent.

## 3. Landlord Entry

At least two days notice except in emergency. Reasonable times. (s. 34-18-26.)

## 4. Eviction notice periods

A notice is not a court order.

| Ground | Typical path |
|--------|----------------|
| Nonpayment | Rent more than 15 days overdue, then a 5-day pay-or-quit (s. 34-18-35) |
| Material lease / health-safety breach | Remedy within 20 days of mailing; termination date not less than 21 days after mailing (s. 34-18-36) |
| Certain criminal / repeat violations | Unconditional quit in listed cases |
| Periodic tenancy, no cause | 30 days month-to-month; 3 months before end of a year-to-year term (s. 34-18-37) |
| Willful holdover | Up to 3 months rent or 3x damages (s. 34-18-38) |

## 5. Illegal evictions

Unlawful exclusion or willful shutoff of heat, water, hot water, electric, or gas: recover possession or terminate and recover up to three months rent or threefold actual damages, whichever is greater, plus fees (s. 34-18-34).

## 6. Retaliation

s. 34-18-46 bars retaliatory rent increases, service cuts, or eviction actions after complaints or tenant-association activity.

## 7. Fair housing

Federal FHA plus RI Human Rights Act classes. Federal lead disclosure for pre-1978 housing.

## 8. Local overlay

Statewide uniformity under s. 34-18-7. Watch city housing codes, short-term rental rules, and lead registries.

## 9. Watch list

P.L. 2024 ch. 243/244 lengthened rent-increase notice. Keep the official Housing handbook current. Confirm NTRAPS / right-to-list recording bans.

## 10. Checklist

1. Deposit at most one month; 20-day itemized return.
2. Rent-increase notices: 60 / 120 days.
3. Two-day entry notice except emergency.
4. Nonpayment: wait 15 days late, then 5-day notice; no lockouts.
5. Fair housing + lead on pre-1978 units.
